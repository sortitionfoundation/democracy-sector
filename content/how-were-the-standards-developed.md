The deliberative standards for decision making were developed over two years by a group of practitioners and representatives of organisations working in the field of deliberation. Development began in December 2024, at the inaugural meeting of the UK Deliberation Network, where three working groups were established, one of which was tasked with working on standards (the other two worked on impact and communications, respectively).

## The need for deliberative standards

Citizens' assemblies in the UK already have an [established set of standards](https://www.involve.org.uk/resources/knowledge-base/how-do-i-setup-citizens-assembly/standards-citizens-assemblies) - but citizens' assemblies are only one of a number of deliberative methods. Deliberative practice is evolving quickly: there is a much broader range of delivery organisations in the space than five years ago, and commissioners are not necessarily looking for a citizens' assembly. With greater supply of, and demand for, deliberation, it is vital there is a set of standards to uphold the quality of deliberative processes and to make clear to commissioners what a good deliberative process involves.

Commissioners do not always understand what a high-quality deliberative process requires, or may lack the time or budget to achieve it. Delivery organisations may nonetheless take on work without adequate resourcing or budget for financial reasons. The 'citizens' assembly' label is being applied to processes that are deliberative in nature but are not assemblies, and as assemblies grow in popularity, more are being delivered poorly. Underlying this is a limited understanding of why standards matter and what is at risk without them. The working group sought to change how decisions about deliberative processes are made, not only to get individual processes right, and recognised this will require some difficult conversations about holding commissioners, and one another, to account. These standards are a tool to support that.

## Existing frameworks to draw on

The [OECD Good Practice Principles](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/innovative-citizen-participation/good-practice-principles-for-deliberative-processes-for-public-decision-making.pdf) cover key areas like accountability and transparency, and there is also an established, co-created set of [standards for citizens' assemblies](https://www.involve.org.uk/resources/knowledge-base/how-do-i-setup-citizens-assembly/standards-citizens-assemblies). We have drawn on these throughout the process and sought to avoid unnecessary duplication. The standards for deliberative decision making are UK-focused in scope.

## The co-creation process

A small group of individuals met regularly between December 2024 and November 2026 to co-create these standards. The vision was to bring in expertise and experience from practitioners, commissioners, participants and evaluators to create standards that respond to the needs of different groups.

This expertise and experience was gathered through a series of roundtables between November 2025 and January 2026. The standards working group then synthesised the outputs from all the roundtables into a draft set of standards, which were tested and refined by roundtable participants and wider sector stakeholders. This happened between February and October 2026.

These standards are designed to be iterative - this first version will serve as a testing ground for putting them into practice. We will review the standards after six months, and annually thereafter.
